How a platformer works

Map data → decoder → objects → physics → drawing

Move: ← → or A / D · Jump: Space / ↑ / W · Restart: R

1. Edit the map

Each character becomes one 40 × 40 pixel cell. # = block, P = player, ^ = spikes, F = finish, / = uphill ramp, \ = downhill ramp, E = walking slime, B = flying bee, . = empty.

Change a row, then build the level. Use 12 rows of equal length (20?200 characters). Wider maps scroll as you move. The decoder checks the data before creating the objects.

2. Follow the files

  • game1/map.js — three levels saved as JSON text.
  • game1/decoder.js — parses the text and turns cells into positioned objects.
  • game1/assets/ — SVG images, separate from the map.
  • game1/enemies.js ? patrol behavior, wall turns, and ledge checks.
  • game1/physics.js — gravity, movement, solid collisions, and ramp surface heights.
  • game1/game.js — controls, hazards, goal, and drawing each frame.

The green image makes a block look solid. Its collision rectangle makes it act solid. Turn off collision boxes to see the difference.

3. Watch the world scroll

Positions in the map stay fixed. The camera subtracts its position when drawing: screenX = worldX ? cameraX. Distant hills move at a fraction of the camera speed, creating depth.

The character changes images to walk, jump, fall, and celebrate. Its collision rectangle stays the same.

Character art: Platformer Characters by Kenney, CC0. Images are included locally, so gameplay needs no internet connection.

4. Ramps and a moving enemy

Walk across the small hill near the start. A ramp calculates a surface height beneath your feet instead of using a flat rectangle. Ramps are one-way: you can jump through their underside.

The slime patrols near its spawn, turns at walls and ledges, and sends you back on contact. Jump over it; this demo has no combat. Try moving E or B in the map. The bee flies back and forth with a gentle bob, ignoring gravity and terrain. All enemy contact resets the level.

Slime walking, bee wingbeats, and flag flutter each alternate between two PNG frames. The artwork comes from Kenney?s New Platformer Pack (CC0), included locally with its license. Animation changes the image, while the collision rectangles stay fixed.
